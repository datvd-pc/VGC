# Day 47 - A Studio Nurture Program Is Not Charity

Stay Hungry. Don't Be Foolish.

The question is not:

"How do we help more studios?"

The better question is:

"How do we build a nurture program that upgrades studio capability and improves future deal quality?"

That is the difference between charity and strategy.

## Past

Many publishers used to treat studio support as ad hoc mentoring.

A few calls.

A few suggestions.

A few introductions.

That is useful at the margin, but it is not enough if the goal is to build a stronger partner base over time.

## Present

The market now needs more structure.

Vietnam has a large pool of young studios, but operating maturity is uneven.

That means a publisher cannot assume good potential will automatically become good execution.

It needs a program that helps the right teams move through a clearer path.

## Evidence

The World Bank's ecosystem work in Vietnam stresses the importance of pipeline quality, early-stage support, and stronger founder capability.

That logic maps directly to publishing.

If early support is random, the pipeline stays weak.

If support is structured, the pipeline improves.

That is why nurture is not overhead.

It is a quality-control layer for future portfolio strength.

Startup ecosystems already understand this.

The best incubators and accelerators are not just places to meet people.

They are systems that improve founder readiness and selection quality.

Publishing should borrow that model.

## Future

Over the next few years, a strong studio nurture program will become a strategic advantage.

Why?

Because it does three things at once:

- It produces better studios.
- It reduces future support cost.
- It improves the quality of the publisher's own selection and retention.

That is compounding.

## Main keyword

**Studio nurture program.**

## What it really means

A nurture program is a structured path for turning promising studios into stronger partners.

It should not be vague.

It should have:

- selection criteria;
- milestones;
- support layers;
- review cadence;
- graduation rule.

Without those, the program becomes branding.

## Scenario

If the nurture program is too loose, it becomes a community activity with no business effect.

If it is too rigid, it becomes bureaucracy that scares good teams away.

If it is well designed, it becomes a capability funnel.

That is the equilibrium you want.

The path should be visible to both sides.

Studios should know what progress looks like.

Publishers should know what support is being exchanged for that progress.

## For publishers

Build a small cohort first.

Do not start with scale.

Start with clarity:

- Who gets in?
- What support do they receive?
- How often do you review progress?
- What counts as graduation?
- What counts as not fit?

## For studios

Treat nurture programs seriously.

They are not just free advice.

They are an opportunity to acquire operating discipline faster than you could alone.

The best studios will use the track to compress learning time, not just collect advice.

## For investors and ecosystem operators

A good nurture program is not cost without return.

It is a pipeline investment.

It improves the quality of the next cohort.

That is what makes it strategic rather than philanthropic.

## Action

In the next 7 days, define a 6 to 8 week nurture track with five components:

- intake;
- milestones;
- support layers;
- review cadence;
- graduation rule.

Then pilot it with a small number of studios.

## Close

The goal is not dependency.

The goal is a stronger partner base.
