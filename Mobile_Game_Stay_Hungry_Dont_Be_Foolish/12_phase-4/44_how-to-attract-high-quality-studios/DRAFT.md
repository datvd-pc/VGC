# Day 44 - High-Quality Studios Choose Useful Publishers

Stay Hungry. Don't Be Foolish.

The question is not:

"How do we make publishers look attractive?"

The better question is:

"What makes a strong studio choose one publisher over another?"

The answer is less glamorous than many people think.

It is not only funding.

It is usefulness.

Vietnam's strongest studios already know that the market is not short of people who want to talk.

It is short of partners who can actually help them move faster.

## Past

In the old model, studios often chose the publisher with the biggest check or the loudest market presence.

That made sense when survival was the first constraint.

If runway is short, money matters a lot.

But the market has matured.

The best teams now know that a bad publishing relationship can cost more than a small check can fix.

## Present

The current environment rewards studios that can think beyond short-term cash.

Studios are asking harder questions:

- Will this publisher understand my game?
- Will they move quickly?
- Will they give direct feedback?
- Will they help me improve the actual product?
- Will they behave fairly after launch?

That is why the publisher's reputation is now a conversion asset.

## Evidence

The research on Vietnamese studios shows a market with many young teams, uneven operating maturity, and gaps in analytics, live ops, and product ownership.

That means the most valuable publisher is not simply the one with money.

It is the one that can reduce those gaps.

This also matches what we see in other Vietnamese ecosystems.

In fintech, trust is the conversion layer.

In e-commerce, seller onboarding and settlement determine whether merchants stay.

In publishing, the same logic applies.

A useful publisher is one that lowers friction and increases the studio's learning velocity.

## Future

Over the next few years, high-quality studios will become more selective.

They will not automatically accept every offer.

They will choose partners who improve:

- decision quality;
- data quality;
- monetization quality;
- live ops quality;
- and long-term optionality.

That means the publisher that wins the best studios will probably not be the loudest.

It will be the most useful.

## Main keyword

**Usefulness.**

## What it really means

Usefulness is not a soft word.

It is operational.

It means the publisher can help with the things that actually move the game:

- product diagnosis;
- creative testing;
- analytics setup;
- monetization review;
- scaling logic;
- post-launch learning.

If the publisher cannot affect those layers, the relationship is mostly financial.

## Scenario

If the publisher offers money but little clarity, the studio may take the deal but not trust the partner.

If the publisher offers clarity, speed, and useful feedback, the studio will treat the partner as strategic.

If the publisher is useful but unfair, the studio will leave when it can.

If the publisher is useful and fair, the relationship becomes durable.

When usefulness is real, the studio stops seeing the publisher as a funding source only.

It starts seeing the publisher as a partner that can change outcomes.

That is a different buying decision.

It also changes word of mouth inside the studio network.

A useful publisher becomes easier to introduce to the next strong team.

## For publishers

Do not try to look impressive first.

Try to be useful first.

That means being able to answer questions like:

- What do you expect from the team?
- What do you give back?
- How fast do you respond?
- How do you handle weak results?
- What happens after launch?

You need a public partner promise.

It should be short, concrete, and testable.

For example:

- we answer within a defined window;
- we give product feedback, not vague encouragement;
- we explain our decision logic;
- we respect the studio's time;
- we review launch learning, not only launch results.

## For studios

Do not evaluate a publisher by surface polish only.

Evaluate them by what changes after you start working together.

If the answer is nothing except money, the deal is thinner than it looks.

If the answer is useful support plus fair process, the relationship has strategic depth.

That depth is what good studios notice.

## For investors and ecosystem operators

The best publishers are not just buyers of inventory.

They are capability multipliers.

That is the kind of operator that can compound.

The useful publisher does not just win one deal.

It lowers the cost of the next deal.

## Action

In the next 7 days, write a one-page partner promise with five lines:

- who you want to work with;
- what support you provide;
- how fast you respond;
- how you review results;
- what the studio should gain from the relationship.

Then use that promise in every studio conversation.

## Close

The best studios do not choose the biggest publisher.

They choose the most useful one.
