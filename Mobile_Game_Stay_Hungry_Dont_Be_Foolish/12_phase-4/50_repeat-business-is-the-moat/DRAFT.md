# Day 50 - Repeat Business Is The Real Moat

Stay Hungry. Don't Be Foolish.

The question is not:

"How do we close the first deal?"

The better question is:

"How do we make the second and third deal easier than the first?"

That is where the moat lives.

## Past

Publishing used to be judged mostly by the first transaction.

Did the deal close?

Did the game launch?

Did the title make money?

That is too narrow.

It ignores the economics of repeat collaboration.

## Present

In the current market, repeat business is more valuable than one-off volume.

Why?

Because the second deal is cheaper to source.

The third deal is faster to evaluate.

The operating mistakes are smaller.

The relationship already has trust.

The publisher already knows how the studio behaves under pressure.

That is a real advantage.

## Evidence

This pattern is already visible in other Vietnamese ecosystems.

E-commerce platforms win when merchants keep selling.

Fintech wins when users keep transacting.

Startup ecosystems win when good founders return, refer others, and re-enter the network.

Publishing is no different.

The strongest publishers are not just moving titles.

They are building recurring relationships with studios that know how to work.

That recurring relationship becomes part of the studio's operating memory.

## Future

Over the next 2 to 4 years, the publishers with the strongest moat will not be the ones with the most one-off deals.

They will be the ones that can generate:

- repeat studio participation;
- repeat collaboration;
- repeat learning;
- repeat revenue;
- repeat trust.

That is much harder to copy.

It also means the publisher spends less energy re-selling itself from zero every time.

## Main keyword

**Repeat business.**

## What it really means

Repeat business is not loyalty as sentiment.

It is loyalty as economics.

The studio comes back because the relationship is still worth more than the alternatives.

That happens when the publisher is:

- fair;
- fast;
- useful;
- predictable;
- and able to create learning.

Those qualities reduce the perceived risk of coming back.

## Scenario

If the publisher treats every project like a one-off, the relationship ends when the title ends.

If the publisher behaves like a long-term operator, the studio sees a future beyond one title.

If the studio learns something real from the relationship, it is more likely to return.

If the ecosystem rewards repeated trust, the entire market becomes more efficient.

## For publishers

Do not optimize only for the first close.

Optimize for the second and third collaboration.

That means tracking:

- who came back;
- who referred someone else;
- who learned enough to work faster next time;
- who became more valuable after the first project.

## For studios

Judge publishers by what happens after launch.

If the relationship continues to create value, it is probably worth keeping.

If the publisher disappears after the first title, the relationship was never that deep.

## For investors and ecosystem operators

Repeat business is a better moat than volume alone.

Volume can be bought.

Repeat trust has to be earned.

## Action

In the next 7 days, track four numbers:

- second-deal rate;
- studio referral rate;
- churn reason;
- repeat partner revenue.

Then review which relationship deserves deeper investment.

## Close

The moat is not the transaction.

The moat is the relationship that survives the transaction.
