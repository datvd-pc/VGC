# Day 41 - Vietnam Needs Publisher Operating Systems, Not More One-Off Deals

Stay Hungry. Don't Be Foolish.

The question is not:

"How do we sign more games?"

The better question is:

"How do we build a publisher operating system that compounds deal flow, trust, learning, and portfolio returns in Vietnam?"

Vietnam already has the raw ingredients for that conversation.

The latest market research points to a country that is unusually strong on mobile game output: around 4.9 billion global downloads in 2025, roughly 95% of them from outside the domestic market, with about 210 active studios and more than 27,000 new games published in the same year.

That is not a small signal.

It means Vietnam is already producing at global scale.

What it does not yet mean is that Vietnam is capturing the most valuable layer of the system.

That is the gap Phase 4 is about.

## Past

For a long time, publishing was treated as a transactional function.

Find a studio.

Fund a title.

Launch it.

Scale if it works.

Move on if it does not.

That model made sense when traffic was cheaper, discovery was easier, and a decent mechanic could still travel far on its own.

It also created a habit: publisher as buyer, studio as supplier.

That framing is now too thin.

## Present

The economics have changed.

Traffic is more expensive.

Attribution is noisier.

Creative fatigue arrives faster.

AI has lowered the cost of making more game variants, which means the market is full of more output and more noise at the same time.

In that environment, the real advantage is not just having capital.

It is having a system that can repeatedly do six things well:

- source the right studios;
- qualify them fast;
- structure the right deal;
- support the right bottlenecks;
- govern the portfolio with discipline;
- keep the strongest partners coming back.

That is a publishing operating system.

## Evidence

The evidence is visible in adjacent ecosystems in Vietnam.

E-commerce does not win because it has more sellers.

It wins because it has seller acquisition, onboarding, trust infrastructure, payment, dispute handling, and settlement mechanics that make repeat participation possible.

Fintech does not win because it has more apps.

It wins because trust, compliance, risk control, and transaction frequency are treated as product design, not back-office noise.

Startup ecosystems do not win because they have more founders.

They win because they build pipeline quality, mentor layers, selection discipline, and capital allocation logic.

Publishing should behave the same way.

And the publisher comparatives already show this logic in the global market.

Voodoo is not only a game buyer. It is a growth system built around selection, creative volume, and portfolio discipline.

TapNation is not only a label. It is a scaled studio network with a strong prototype pipeline.

SayGames sells long-term collaboration and deeper partnership, not just a one-off launch.

Unity Supersonic is tightly integrated with a platform and growth infrastructure.

These are different operating models, not just different brand names.

## Future

Over the next 2 to 4 years, the best publishers in Vietnam will not be the ones with the loudest presence.

They will be the ones that can do three things repeatedly:

1. Convert market access into high-quality deal flow.
2. Convert deal flow into better studio capability.
3. Convert capability into repeat business and portfolio compounding.

That is how a publisher moves from buyer to orchestrator.

## Main keyword

**Operating model.**

## What it really means

A publisher operating model is not a spreadsheet.

It is a set of behaviors.

It decides:

- who gets attention;
- who gets feedback;
- who gets support;
- who gets capital;
- who gets escalated;
- who gets invited back.

If those decisions are not explicit, the publisher is not really operating a model.

It is reacting to the market.

## Scenario

If the publisher behaves like a volume buyer, the studio will behave like a vendor.

That creates shallow relationships, weak retention, and low learning transfer.

If the publisher behaves like a platform, the studio will behave like a partner.

That creates better signal, better retention, and stronger repeat business.

If the ecosystem lacks this layer entirely, Vietnam can still become a production base.

But it will be a production base that exports output more easily than it captures value.

## For publishers

The right question is not whether you have enough games in the funnel.

The right question is whether the funnel itself is making you smarter.

You should be able to show:

- what kind of studio you are trying to attract;
- what you reject quickly;
- what support you actually provide;
- how learning is retained after launch;
- why a good studio would come back.

## For studios

Do not evaluate a publisher only by the check size.

Evaluate them by whether they help you increase decision quality:

- better data;
- better creative testing;
- better monetization thinking;
- better live ops discipline;
- better access to distribution.

That is where a strong publisher becomes worth staying with.

## For investors and ecosystem operators

Do not treat publishing as a simple intermediary business.

Treat it as an operating system business.

The difference matters because the second model compounds.

## Action

In the next 7 days, map your publishing model into six layers:

- sourcing;
- qualification;
- deal design;
- support;
- governance;
- retention.

Then ask one hard question for each layer:

what gets better because this layer exists?

## Close

Publishing is no longer just about buying games.

It is about designing the system that makes good studios easier to find, easier to support, and easier to keep.
