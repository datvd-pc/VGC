# Day 45 - Studio Selection Is Portfolio Governance

Stay Hungry. Don't Be Foolish.

The question is not:

"Is this studio good?"

The better question is:

"What role should this studio play in the publisher's portfolio, and how much capital, support, and attention should it receive?"

That is a governance question, not a sales question.

## Past

Many publishers used to select studios mainly through instinct.

A good pitch.

A promising demo.

A founder who seemed hungry.

That model is fragile.

It works until the portfolio gets larger, the cost of mistakes rises, and the support burden starts to matter.

## Present

Today, selection is more like capital allocation.

The publisher has to decide:

- Which studios deserve a pilot?
- Which studios deserve nurture?
- Which studios deserve deeper investment?
- Which studios should be paused?

Research on Vietnam's studio landscape shows a young ecosystem with uneven maturity.

That makes selection quality even more important.

One weak decision can consume time, capital, and internal attention that should have gone to a better team.

## Evidence

The broader publishing market already demonstrates this logic.

Voodoo has publicly described moving from a very wide studio network toward a much smaller, more selective partner base.

TapNation has built scale through a large studio network and a high prototype cadence.

SayGames emphasizes deeper partnership and long-term collaboration.

These are different portfolio philosophies.

The common thread is that all of them treat studio selection as a strategic filter.

That is the important point.

## Future

Over the next few years, the best publishers will not simply ask whether a studio can produce.

They will ask:

- Can this team absorb feedback?
- Can it improve after each test?
- Can it operate with discipline?
- Can it create repeat value?
- Does it fit the portfolio strategy?

This is why studio selection becomes governance.

The right selection process creates better returns.

The wrong one creates hidden operating cost.

## Main keyword

**Portfolio governance.**

## What it really means

Portfolio governance is the discipline of matching the right studio to the right level of support.

It prevents two common errors:

1. Over-supporting a team that is not ready.
2. Under-supporting a team that deserves more capital and attention.

When governance is weak, the publisher confuses activity with strategic progress.

## Scenario

If the publisher treats every studio equally, the portfolio becomes noisy.

If the publisher uses a clear scorecard, the portfolio becomes legible.

If the publisher allocates support by stage, the portfolio becomes more efficient.

If the publisher confuses charm with maturity, the portfolio becomes expensive.

The point is not to be harsh.

The point is to be precise.

Precision lets the publisher allocate better, and it helps the studio understand what it must prove next.

## For publishers

Define four buckets:

- Pilot candidate;
- Nurture candidate;
- Strategic partner;
- Not now.

Then define what each bucket gets:

- capital;
- response speed;
- internal attention;
- support layers;
- review cadence.

Do not overcomplicate it.

Do make it explicit.

Explicit buckets do three things.

They reduce internal ambiguity.

They reduce emotional drift.

They make the trade-off visible instead of hidden.

## For studios

Understand the bucket you are in.

Do not ask for strategic-partner support if your signal is still pilot-level.

Do not mistake rejection for disrespect.

Sometimes the answer is simply stage mismatch.

That is useful information, not a personal judgment.

It tells the studio which proof is missing.

It tells the publisher where the next bet should or should not happen.

## For investors and ecosystem operators

A healthy portfolio is not a random collection of teams.

It is a controlled set of bets with different stages and different support intensity.

That is how real compounding happens.

The best portfolios are not emotional collections of teams.

They are controlled sets of bets with different support intensity.

## Action

In the next 7 days, build a simple portfolio board with four columns:

- Pilot;
- Nurture;
- Strategic partner;
- Not now.

Then assign one reason for every studio in the system.

If the reason cannot be explained in one sentence, the selection logic is probably too loose.

## Close

Choosing studios is how publishers manage capital.

That is why it is portfolio governance.
