# Publisher Operating Model in Vietnam - Phase 4 Research

## 1. Thesis

Phase 4 is not about running more campaigns or adding more partners.
It is about designing a publisher operating model that can repeatedly attract, evaluate, convert, support, and retain high-quality studios in Vietnam.

The relevant question is no longer:

> How do we publish more games?

The better question is:

> How do we build a publishing system that makes studio acquisition, capability transfer, and portfolio returns repeatable?

## 2. The closest Vietnamese analogues

### 2.1 E-commerce platforms

What works:

- Seller acquisition is treated as a pipeline, not a one-off sales motion;
- Onboarding is standardized;
- Trust is operationalized through payments, dispute handling, ratings, and service recovery;
- Logistics and settlement are part of the product, not back-office noise;
- Platform economics depend on repeated participation, not isolated transactions.

Vietnam relevance:

- VECOM's 2025 report describes Vietnamese e-commerce as entering a new stage of rapid and sustainable development, with 2024 online retail size around 32B USD and annual growth around 27%.
- This matters because e-commerce in Vietnam shows how platform businesses win through distribution, trust, and operational discipline.

Publisher implication:

- Studio acquisition is the seller pipeline;
- Deal structure is onboarding;
- Milestone delivery is settlement;
- Post-launch support is dispute handling;
- Reputation is the platform's trust layer.

Source:

- https://en.vecom.vn/vietnam-e-business-report-2025

### 2.2 Fintech and payments

What works:

- Trust is the product;
- Compliance and risk control are structural, not optional;
- Frequency of use matters more than one-time conversion;
- Distribution and retention reinforce each other;
- Operational reliability is a moat.

Vietnam relevance:

- World Bank research on Vietnam's innovation ecosystem highlights that two of the country's unicorns are in e-payments, which is useful as a signal that trust-heavy, transaction-heavy businesses can scale in Vietnam.
- Payments businesses in Vietnam have demonstrated that repeated usage, user trust, and risk management can become durable commercial advantages.

Publisher implication:

- Payout discipline matters;
- Contract clarity matters;
- Data access and reporting cadence matter;
- Publisher reputation becomes a conversion asset.

Sources:

- https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2024/04/23/viet-nam-s-economy-poised-for-gradual-recovery-world-bank-report-finds
- https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/eastasiapacific/why-viet-nam-needs-more-innovative-entrepreneurs-

### 2.3 Startup ecosystem / incubator model

What works:

- Pipeline creation is deliberate;
- Early-stage capital is used to buy learning, not just output;
- Mentors and operators reduce failure rates;
- Screening criteria become sharper over time;
- Ecosystem institutions win when they improve the quality of investable or scalable companies.

Vietnam relevance:

- The World Bank diagnostic on innovative entrepreneurship in Vietnam emphasizes pipeline quality, access to early-stage finance, and stronger ecosystem support programs.
- This is the closest institutional analogue to a publisher that wants repeatable deal flow and better partner quality.

Publisher implication:

- Publisher is not only a buyer of games;
- Publisher is also a curator, allocator, and capability builder;
- The best studios should be treated as portfolio assets, not disposable vendors.

Source:

- https://documents.worldbank.org/en/publication/documents-reports/documentdetail/099111623132511499

## 3. What carries over into publishing

### 3.1 Pipeline is a system

Publisher growth depends on a reliable pipeline:

- Sourcing;
- Qualification;
- Evaluation;
- Pilot;
- Soft launch;
- Scale;
- Retention.

### 3.2 Trust is monetized

Studios return when the publisher is seen as:

- Fast;
- Explicit;
- Fair;
- Predictable;
- Useful after the deal closes.

### 3.3 Capability transfer compounds

A publisher becomes strategically stronger when each engagement leaves the studio better at:

- Analytics;
- Monetization;
- Live ops;
- Creative iteration;
- Product thinking;
- Operating cadence.

### 3.4 Portfolio governance matters more than title-level excitement

Strong publishers know:

- Not every title deserves the same capital;
- Not every studio deserves the same support level;
- Not every event should exist for branding;
- Not every lead should enter the pipeline.

## 4. Scenario map

### Scenario A: Volume-first publisher

Goal:

- Maximize partner inflow and prototype count.

Risk:

- Weak filtering;
- Operational fatigue;
- Low-quality portfolio inflation.

### Scenario B: Focused publisher

Goal:

- Work with fewer studios, but increase depth of support and decision quality.

Risk:

- Narrower pipeline;
- Overdependence on a small number of teams.

### Scenario C: Platform-style publisher

Goal:

- Build repeatable acquisition, evaluation, support, and retention loops.

Risk:

- Higher operating overhead;
- Requires stronger internal discipline.

### Scenario D: Capability-transfer publisher

Goal:

- Improve studio quality over time so the same ecosystem produces better output with less friction.

Risk:

- Slower short-term monetization;
- Requires patience and strong governance.

## 6. Working conclusion

Phase 4 should use the language of operating systems, not campaign execution.

The appropriate vocabulary is:

- Ecosystem development;
- Deal flow architecture;
- Studio enablement;
- Capability transfer;
- Portfolio governance;
- Retention economics;
- Trust infrastructure;
- Operating cadence.

That is the level where a publisher in Vietnam starts to look durable rather than merely active.
