# Vietnam Needs Publisher Operating Systems, Not More One-Off Deals

Stay Hungry. Don't Be Foolish.

The question is not:

"How do we sign more games?"

The better question is:

"How do we build a Publisher Operating System that compounds Deal Flow, trust, learning, and Portfolio returns in Vietnam?"

Vietnam already has the raw signal for this conversation.

Recent market research points to Vietnam as a serious Mobile Game production market: about 4.9 billion downloads in 2025, roughly 95% of them from outside Vietnam, around 210 active Studios, and more than 27,000 new games published.

That is not a small signal.

It means Vietnam is already participating in the global game economy.

But output is not the same as value capture.

Downloads prove reach.

They do not prove control.

They do not prove that Studios own the Data, the IP, the learning, the Monetization system, or the partner relationship.

They also do not prove that foreign Publishers have a repeatable way to discover, qualify, support, and retain the right Vietnamese teams.

That is the real opportunity.

Vietnam does not lack production talent.

Vietnam lacks enough repeatable operating systems around that talent.

## Past

For a long time, publishing could work as a transaction.

Find a Studio.

Fund a title.

Test it.

Scale it if the metrics work.

Move on if it fails.

That model made sense when traffic was cheaper, discovery was easier, and a good mechanic could still travel further on its own.

It also created a simple relationship:

Publisher as buyer.

Studio as supplier.

For many Vietnamese teams, this was rational. A Studio with limited runway often needs Cashflow more than theory. A deal that pays the team, keeps salaries stable, and gives a shot at global launch can be attractive.

But the same model also creates a ceiling.

The Studio may ship output without building Operating Maturity.

The Publisher may receive prototypes without building local trust.

Both sides may finish the project without becoming much smarter for the next one.

## Present

The market has changed.

Traffic is more expensive.

Attribution is noisier.

Creative fatigue arrives faster.

AI has reduced the cost of creating more variants, which means the market now has more output and more noise at the same time.

In this environment, capital is still important, but capital alone is not enough.

The stronger advantage is the ability to repeatedly do six things well:

1. Source the right Studios.
2. Qualify them fast.
3. Structure the right deal.
4. Support the real bottleneck.
5. Govern the Portfolio with discipline.
6. Keep the strongest partners coming back.

That is a Publisher Operating System.

It is not only a CRM.

It is not only a BD list.

It is not only a UA budget.

It is the set of decisions that define who gets attention, who gets feedback, who gets support, who gets capital, who gets escalated, and who gets invited back.

If those decisions are not explicit, the Publisher is not operating a system.

It is reacting to the market.

## Evidence

The logic is visible in other Vietnamese ecosystems.

E-commerce did not grow only because there were more sellers.

It grew because platforms built seller acquisition, onboarding, trust infrastructure, payment, dispute handling, settlement, and repeat behavior.

Fintech and payments did not scale only because there were more apps.

They scaled because trust, reliability, risk control, and frequency became part of the product.

Startup ecosystems do not become stronger only because there are more Founders.

They become stronger when pipeline quality, mentoring, early capital, selection discipline, and follow-on support improve.

Publishing in Vietnam should be read in the same way.

The question is not whether there are enough teams.

The question is whether someone can turn those teams into a compounding partner network.

Global Publisher comparatives already show different operating logics.

Voodoo is not only a buyer of games. It is a growth machine with strong selection and creative volume.

TapNation is not only a label. It is a scaled Studio network with a strong prototype pipeline.

SayGames often signals deeper long-term collaboration rather than pure one-off launch.

Unity Supersonic connects publishing with platform and growth infrastructure.

These are not just different brand names.

They are different operating models.

## Future

Over the next 2-4 years, I do not think the best Publisher in Vietnam will be the one with the loudest event booth or the biggest one-time check.

The best Publisher will be the one that can turn local access into a repeatable advantage.

That means:

- Better Deal Flow;
- Faster qualification;
- Clearer deals;
- Stronger Studio capability;
- Better Portfolio allocation;
- Higher repeat partner rate.

The formula is:

> Production output creates reach.  
> Operating Model creates leverage.

**Operating Model.**

That is the keyword.

If a Publisher behaves like a volume buyer, the Studio will behave like a vendor.

The relationship becomes shallow.

Feedback becomes defensive.

Data becomes fragmented.

The second deal is not easier than the first.

If a Publisher behaves like a platform, the Studio can behave like a partner.

The relationship becomes clearer.

Feedback becomes useful.

Learning becomes reusable.

The second deal becomes faster, cheaper, and more rational.

## For Publishers

Do not only ask whether you have enough games in the funnel.

Ask whether the funnel itself is making you smarter.

You should be able to answer:

- What type of Studio do we want?
- What type of Studio do we reject quickly?
- What signal must exist before we spend senior time?
- What support do we actually provide?
- How do we preserve learning after launch?
- Why would a strong Studio return?

If these answers are unclear, Vietnam will feel noisy.

The market is not necessarily the problem.

Your interface with the market may be the problem.

There is another reason this matters.

Vietnamese Studios often do not show their full quality in the first interaction.

Some teams are technically good but weak at pitching.

Some Founders are careful because they have seen unclear deals before.

Some teams can execute well but cannot explain their decision process in the language a global Publisher expects.

If your system only rewards polished presentation, you may miss strong Operators.

If your system only rewards cheap production, you may attract the wrong behavior.

The Operating System should help you read beneath the surface.

It should separate:

- Weak team, weak pitch;
- Strong team, weak pitch;
- Weak team, strong pitch;
- Strong team, strong pitch.

The second category is where a Vietnam Operator can create real value.

That is often where local interpretation matters.

## For Studios

Do not evaluate a Publisher only by check size.

Evaluate whether the relationship improves your decision quality.

Does the Publisher help you understand Data better?

Does it improve Creative Testing?

Does it make Monetization less random?

Does it clarify legal, IP, and payout expectations?

Does it make your next game better?

If not, you may have received a deal but not a capability upgrade.

That matters because the Vietnamese Studio that learns faster will become more valuable than the Studio that only ships faster.

If I were a Vietnamese Founder, I would not only ask for better revenue share.

I would ask for better learning rights.

Can we see the test Data?

Can we join the post-test review?

Can we understand why a creative won or failed?

Can we reuse the learning in another title?

Can we build internal capability from this collaboration?

If the answer is no, the deal may still be useful for Cashflow.

But I would not confuse it with strategic growth.

## For Investors And Ecosystem Operators

Do not treat publishing as a simple intermediary business.

Treat it as an Operating System business.

The second model can compound.

It can build trust, pipeline, Studio maturity, Data memory, and repeat economics.

That is why a serious Vietnam strategy should not stop at "we know some teams."

It should ask:

What system turns access into advantage?

That system also decides what kind of local role is needed.

A foreign Publisher can bring capital, Data, legal strength, UA knowledge, IP experience, platform relationships, and Portfolio discipline.

But Vietnam still needs someone who can read local behavior:

- When a Studio says yes but has not understood;
- When a Founder is capable but cautious;
- When a team is good at production but not ready for ownership;
- When a Cashflow-first decision is rational;
- When a weak-looking team has strong learning speed.

This is not soft culture talk.

It is Operating Risk.

The Publisher that reads these signals faster will waste less capital.

The Publisher that ignores them will need more deals to find the same quality.

## 7-Day Action

Map your current publishing model into six layers:

1. Sourcing.
2. Qualification.
3. Deal Design.
4. Support.
5. Governance.
6. Retention.

For each layer, answer one hard question:

What becomes faster, clearer, or more valuable because this layer exists?

If the answer is vague, the layer is not real yet.
