# How To Attract High-Quality Studios

Stay Hungry. Don't Be Foolish.

The question is not:

"How do we convince more good Studios to work with us?"

The better question is:

"What must our Publisher promise be so a strong Vietnamese Studio sees us as an upgrade, not just another buyer?"

Many Publishers think Studio attraction begins with money.

Money matters.

Revenue share matters.

UA budget matters.

Minimum guarantees can matter.

But strong Studios do not choose partners only by check size.

They choose by leverage.

They ask, directly or indirectly:

Will this Publisher make our next decision better?

Will this relationship improve our Data, creative process, Monetization thinking, Live Ops discipline, legal clarity, or market access?

Will we learn something that stays inside the Studio after the deal?

Will the Publisher respond quickly and clearly?

Will the contract be fair enough that we can focus on the game instead of protecting ourselves from ambiguity?

If the answer is unclear, the Studio may still take the deal.

But it may not return.

## Past

In the earlier stage of the market, a Publisher could attract many Studios with three things:

- Access to global distribution;
- UA money;
- The possibility of a hit.

For many Vietnamese teams, that was enough.

The Studio needed Cashflow.

The Founder needed a chance.

The team needed proof that its work could travel outside Vietnam.

The Publisher controlled the layers that were hard for local teams to access: traffic, Data, Monetization, platform relationships, legal support, and market experience.

That structure still exists.

But the stronger Studios are becoming more selective.

## Present

Vietnam has many production-capable teams, but maturity is uneven.

Some Studios are strong at engineering and weak at analytics.

Some are fast at art production but weak at product ownership.

Some understand Puzzle craft but do not understand IAP depth.

Some can polish well but cannot run disciplined Live Ops.

Some Founders are commercially sharp but do not yet know how to negotiate IP, Data access, and long-term upside.

This creates a real opportunity for Publishers.

But only if the Publisher can become useful.

**Studio Value Proposition.**

That is the keyword.

A strong Studio Value Proposition has five factors:

1. Fairness.
2. Speed.
3. Useful feedback.
4. Learning access.
5. Deal clarity.

Without these, the Publisher looks like another buyer.

With these, the Publisher becomes a partner worth returning to.

## Evidence

The best analogy is not only gaming.

It is trust-heavy platform business.

In fintech and payments, trust is not a slogan. It is built through reliability, transparency, risk control, and repeated successful transactions.

In e-commerce, sellers return to platforms that help them sell, get paid, resolve disputes, and understand performance.

Publishing works the same way.

A Studio returns when the Publisher helps it improve its chance of winning.

Global Publisher models also show different attraction promises.

Some Publishers attract by speed and volume.

Some attract by platform infrastructure.

Some attract by deeper collaboration.

Some attract by brand, capital, and proven scaling capacity.

Vietnamese Studios need to understand those differences.

Foreign Publishers entering Vietnam need to make their own promise explicit.

## Future

Over the next 2-4 years, I expect strong Vietnamese Studios to become harder to attract with generic publishing language.

"We can publish your game globally" will not be enough.

"We have UA budget" will not be enough.

"We are looking for partners in Vietnam" will not be enough.

The better Studios will ask:

- How fast do you review?
- What feedback will we receive?
- What Data can we see?
- What support do you provide before and after launch?
- What happens if the first game fails?
- What rights do we keep?
- What would make you invest more in us?

Those questions are healthy.

They show that the Studio is becoming more mature.

## For Publishers

Write your partner promise before you ask for trust.

The promise should answer:

- Who you work with;
- What types of games you want;
- What you provide;
- What you do not provide;
- How submissions are reviewed;
- How fast Studios receive a response;
- What happens after rejection;
- What happens after a successful pilot;
- What a long-term partner can expect.

This promise does not need to reveal confidential strategy.

It needs to reduce uncertainty.

Vietnamese teams often work better when expectations are explicit.

If the Publisher is vague, the Studio may say yes politely but misunderstand the real standard.

That creates friction later.

The promise should also say what the Publisher will not do.

This is important.

Some Publishers overpromise support because they want to attract more teams.

Then the Studio expects deep product help, fast review, UA budget, creative support, Monetization advice, and long-term care.

The Publisher may only intend to offer testing and launch support.

That mismatch creates disappointment.

In Vietnam, disappointment is not always expressed directly.

The Studio may become slower.

The Founder may avoid hard conversations.

The team may say yes in meetings but not fully commit.

The relationship weakens before either side names the problem.

Clear boundaries are part of the value proposition.

## For Studios

Do not judge a Publisher only by the headline offer.

Judge the Operating Experience.

Ask:

- Did they respond clearly?
- Did they understand our game?
- Did their feedback improve our thinking?
- Did they explain the next step?
- Did they respect our time?
- Did they clarify Data, IP, and commercial terms?
- Did the process make us stronger?

If the answer is yes, the relationship may be worth more than a slightly better first deal elsewhere.

If the answer is no, be careful.

Money without learning can become a short-term comfort.

I would also advise Studios to separate three kinds of offers:

1. Cashflow offer.
2. Learning offer.
3. Strategic growth offer.

A Cashflow offer helps the team survive.

A learning offer improves the next project.

A strategic growth offer changes the company's position.

Do not pretend they are the same.

Taking Cashflow work is not shameful.

But if you call every Cashflow deal strategic, you will not know why the Studio is not growing.

The right question is:

What do we gain after the money is spent?

## For Advisors And Operators

The biggest hidden work is translation.

Not only language translation.

Expectation translation.

A foreign Publisher may think a requirement is obvious.

A Vietnamese Studio may think silence means rejection, or politeness means agreement, or a casual comment means a final decision.

The Operator must make the relationship readable.

That means:

- Written notes;
- Decision rules;
- Response deadlines;
- Contract explanation;
- Feedback structure;
- Escalation path.

Clarity is not administrative overhead.

It is a trust product.

This is also where AI and tools can help.

A Publisher can use structured forms, CRM notes, automated reminders, meeting summaries, and shared decision logs.

A Studio can use AI to prepare English summaries, clarify questions, translate feedback into tasks, and maintain a learning log.

But tools should not replace judgment.

They should reduce misunderstanding.

The real goal is simple:

No important expectation should live only in someone's memory.

If the relationship is valuable, write it down.

## Decision Rule

A Publisher is attractive when a strong Studio can explain the next step without guessing.

That is the test.

After the first two meetings, the Studio should know:

- What the Publisher wants;
- What standard the game must pass;
- What Data matters;
- What support is real;
- What timeline applies;
- What commercial topics need discussion;
- What would make the Publisher say no.

If the Studio cannot explain these points, the Publisher may have brand awareness but not operating clarity.

In Vietnam, operating clarity is part of attraction because it reduces hidden risk for Founders.

## 7-Day Action

Write a Studio Value Proposition.

Use five sections:

1. The Studios we want.
2. The support we provide.
3. The decisions we make.
4. The timelines we follow.
5. The reasons strong Studios should return.

Then convert it into a scorecard:

- Fairness;
- Speed;
- Useful Feedback;
- Learning Access;
- Deal Clarity.

Score yourself honestly.

The weak score tells you where your Vietnam strategy will leak trust.

Good Studios do not only ask who can publish them.

Final operating note:

If I were helping a foreign Publisher enter Vietnam, I would audit the first two Studio touchpoints before touching the deal terms.

The first email, the first call, the first review note, and the first rejection already tell the market what kind of partner you are.

Attraction begins before negotiation.

Strong Studios notice process quality early.
