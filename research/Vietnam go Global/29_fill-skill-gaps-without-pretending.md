# Fill Skill Gaps Without Pretending

Everyone has skill gaps.
The real difference is whether they know how to fill them.

In game work, many people are missing the same categories:

- AI usage;
- Pipeline understanding;
- Product insight;
- Thinking discipline;
- Working style;
- Communication.

The problem is not only that the gaps exist.
The problem is that many people try to hide them instead of building a system to close them.

That never ends well.

The formula is:

> Learn = read -> note -> compare -> test -> reflect -> update playbook

That is the loop.

For a junior Designer, skill gaps may look like this:

- You can design a mechanic, but do you understand trust?
- You can make a level difficult, but do you understand fairness?
- You can place a reward, but do you understand timing?

For a PM, skill gaps may look like this:

- You can move tasks, but can you make a decision trail?
- You can organize a team, but can you escalate risk?
- You can run meetings, but can you reduce ambiguity?

For a Developer, skill gaps may look like this:

- You can code, but can you read analytics?
- You can ship, but can you see why a crash matters?
- You can fix bugs, but can you understand the product context?

AI can help here, but only if you use it as a learning assistant.

AI is useful for:

- Summarizing reports;
- Explaining terms;
- Generating flashcards;
- Comparing options;
- Building a 30-day learning plan.

AI is not useful if you use it to pretend you understand things you do not.

If I were a young Designer, I would learn FAR, APS, SR, fairness, and near-miss before I learned fancy buzzwords.
If I were a PM, I would learn decision logging and escalation before I learned more frameworks.
If I were a Founder, I would learn Cashflow and deal literacy before I tried to look strategic.

In the next 2-4 years, the people who build learning loops will outgrow the people who only collect information.

Learning is a system.
Not a mood.

The smartest people do not pretend the gap is not there.
They turn the gap into a training plan.

For young Vietnamese game talent, there are several common gaps.

**AI Gap**

Many people have tried AI tools, but still use them like search boxes or idea generators. That is too shallow.

Better use:

- Turn messy notes into structured decisions;
- Compare competitor onboarding flows;
- Draft edge-case checklists;
- Simulate player personas;
- Summarize market reports;
- Create first-pass economy assumptions;
- Rewrite unclear English updates;
- Generate questions before a Publisher meeting.

The rule:

> AI should improve your thinking process, not replace your thinking.

**Pipeline Gap**

Some teams work hard but have no repeatable production system. Every project starts from scratch. Every build has different naming. Every event map is improvised. Every postmortem looks different.

That creates hidden cost.

A better pipeline includes:

- Prototype template;
- Event naming convention;
- Build checklist;
- QA checklist;
- Level review checklist;
- Creative test format;
- Meeting note format;
- Postmortem template.

This is not bureaucracy.
This is how small teams become faster.

**Insight Gap**

A lot of juniors can describe what they built, but not why it matters.

Insight means connecting work to behavior:

- Why would the player understand this mechanic?
- Why would they return on day 7?
- Why would this ad promise attract traffic?
- Why would this economy create pressure without frustration?
- Why would this Live Ops event change behavior?

If you cannot connect your task to player behavior, you are still thinking too locally.

**Communication Gap**

Many misunderstandings happen because people wait too long to ask.

The fix:

- Ask questions before starting;
- Write assumptions;
- Confirm decisions;
- Expose risks early;
- Summarize after meetings.

This one habit can improve your value quickly.

**Positioning Gap**

Some people do not know what role they are actually playing.

A Developer may need to become a technical partner, not just a task finisher.
A Designer may need to become a product thinker, not just an idea person.
A PM may need to become a decision Operator, not just a scheduler.
A Founder may need to become a capital allocator, not just a strong executor.

If you do not know your position, you cannot choose the right skill to learn.

My recommendation:

Pick one gap for 30 days.

Not five.
One.

For 30 days, build proof:

- One better template;
- One clearer document;
- One better postmortem;
- One competitor breakdown;
- One AI-assisted workflow;
- One meeting recap system;
- One Data reading habit.

Then show the proof.

In this market, the people who grow fastest will not be the people who claim to know everything. They will be the people who close one important gap at a time and make that improvement visible.

If you want a simple system, use a 3-column note:

| Gap | Action | Proof |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AI | Use AI to clean meeting notes | Better recaps, fewer misunderstandings |
| Data | Learn one metric and one decision | Better design review |
| Communication | Write pre-meeting brief | Faster alignment |
| Pipeline | Create one reusable template | Less friction on the next project |
| Positioning | Define your current role honestly | Better career choices |

That table is simple, but it forces focus.

It stops the common mistake of “learning more” without changing behavior.

The point is not to become impressive.
The point is to become more useful in the specific market you are in.

That is how skill growth turns into leverage.
