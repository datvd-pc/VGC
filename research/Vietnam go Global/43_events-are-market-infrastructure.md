# Events Are Market Infrastructure

Stay Hungry. Don't Be Foolish.

The question is not:

"Should we sponsor or attend more game events in Vietnam?"

The better question is:

"How do we turn events into qualified signal, follow-up discipline, pilot opportunities, and repeat relationships?"

Events are easy to misunderstand.

They look like branding.

They look like networking.

They look like panels, photos, booths, speaker slots, and social media posts.

All of that can be useful.

But for a Publisher entering Vietnam, that is not the real value.

The real value of an event is market memory.

Who did you meet?

What signal did you capture?

Which Studios are qualified?

Which teams need nurture?

Which Founders are serious but not ready?

Which conversations should become pilots?

Which introductions should become repeat relationships?

If the event cannot answer those questions, it is not market infrastructure.

It is a temporary room.

## Past

In an early ecosystem, events often begin as visibility tools.

People need to know each other.

Studios need access.

Publishers need local presence.

Founders need a place to compare notes.

That stage matters.

Vietnam's game ecosystem is still relationship-driven in many places. A warm introduction can open a door faster than a cold email. A real conversation can reveal more than a deck. A local event can help foreign partners understand tone, confidence, skill gaps, and ambition.

But the old event logic has a weakness.

Attendance becomes the metric.

Photos become the proof.

Panels become the output.

Everyone feels active, but the market does not necessarily get smarter.

## Present

The bottleneck is no longer only awareness.

The bottleneck is trust and qualification.

A foreign Publisher may meet 30 teams but not know which 5 deserve follow-up.

A Studio may talk to 10 Publishers but not know who is serious.

An organizer may host a successful room but not know whether any business moved forward.

That is why events need an operating funnel.

**Signal Capture.**

That is the keyword.

The event funnel has five stages:

1. Attendance.
2. Qualified lead.
3. Structured follow-up.
4. Pilot.
5. Repeat contact or repeat deal.

The event itself is only stage one.

The real value begins after the room closes.

## Evidence

This is how other ecosystems work.

In e-commerce, seller events are not valuable only because many sellers attend.

They are valuable if they feed onboarding, trust, payment, service standards, and repeat transactions.

In startup ecosystems, demo days are not valuable only because Founders pitch.

They are valuable if they create investable follow-up, mentor matching, capital conversations, or better company selection.

Publishing events should be judged the same way.

Not:

"How many people came?"

But:

"What did the ecosystem learn, and what business can happen next?"

For Vietnam, this is especially important because some strong Studios are quiet. They may not pitch aggressively. They may not have perfect English materials. They may not show their best thinking in a casual booth conversation.

A good event system helps reveal them.

A bad event system rewards only the loudest teams.

This is one reason a local Operator matters.

At an event, the best signal is not always visible on stage.

Sometimes it appears in how a Founder explains a failed launch.

Sometimes it appears in how a Designer talks about player behavior.

Sometimes it appears in how a Developer describes technical debt.

Sometimes it appears in whether a team admits what it does not know.

Foreign Publishers may miss these signals if they only read polished materials.

Vietnamese teams may also miss the Publisher's real expectations if no one translates the standard clearly.

The event should reduce this gap.

Not widen it.

## Future

Over the next 2-4 years, Vietnam will likely see more game events, more Publisher visits, more partnership panels, and more ecosystem activity.

That is good.

But it will also create event inflation.

More activity does not automatically mean more advantage.

The winners will be the Operators who build post-event memory.

They will track:

- Who attended;
- What each Studio brought;
- What signal was captured;
- Who owns follow-up;
- What deadline exists;
- What pilot criteria apply;
- What learning returns to the system.

The market will reward the people who convert events into infrastructure.

The weak version of an event creates attention for one day.

The strong version creates a database of market truth.

That database should answer:

- Which Studios are production-ready;
- Which Studios are publishing-ready;
- Which Studios need a nurture program;
- Which roles are missing in the local talent pool;
- Which topics need training;
- Which Publisher promises are attractive;
- Which concerns stop Studios from moving forward.

If the event does not produce this learning, the ecosystem has spent energy but not built memory.

## For Publishers

Do not attend a Vietnam event without a follow-up system.

Before the event, define what you are looking for.

Are you looking for:

- Prototype teams;
- Puzzle/Hybrid Puzzle specialists;
- Live Ops talent;
- Art production partners;
- Cashflow Studios;
- Strategic long-term partners;
- Local PM or BD talent?

Each target requires different questions.

At the event, do not only collect business cards.

Collect signal.

Ask:

- What have you shipped?
- What did you learn from the last test?
- What metric do you understand best?
- What bottleneck blocks your next step?
- What kind of Publisher support do you actually need?

After the event, send a clear next step.

That is where trust starts.

## For Studios

Do not attend events only to be seen.

Attend to become easier to evaluate.

Prepare:

- A short Studio profile;
- One playable;
- One metric snapshot;
- One clear ask;
- One honest weakness;
- One follow-up email.

If your English is not perfect, write better notes.

If your deck is not polished, make your thinking clear.

If your game is early, explain what you are trying to learn.

Strong partners do not need you to pretend.

They need you to be readable.

If I were a Vietnamese Studio attending a Publisher event, I would prepare one page before the meeting:

- What we have shipped;
- What we are good at;
- What we are not yet good at;
- What type of partner we want;
- What decision we need after this meeting;
- What we can send within 48 hours.

Then I would send a summary email after the meeting.

Not a long email.

A clear one:

- What we discussed;
- What we understood;
- What we will send;
- What question remains;
- Who owns the next step.

This simple habit makes the Studio look more global-ready.

## For Ecosystem Operators

Design the event around conversion quality.

A strong event should have:

- Pre-event qualification;
- Structured meeting slots;
- Shared intake format;
- Post-event CRM;
- Follow-up deadline;
- Pilot tracking;
- Learning summary.

This is not only useful for Publishers.

It helps Studios understand what global partners expect.

It helps Investors see which teams are real.

It helps the ecosystem move from noise to signal.

If I were organizing a Vietnam game event for foreign Publishers, I would avoid designing only for audience size.

I would design for conversion.

That means smaller curated rooms may be more valuable than large generic panels.

It also means every sponsor package should include a post-event signal report:

- Number of relevant Studios;
- Category breakdown;
- Maturity distribution;
- Follow-up status;
- Top repeated questions;
- Top capability gaps.

That report is more useful than photos.

## 7-Day Action

Before the next event, build an event follow-up pipeline.

Use five columns:

1. Attended.
2. Qualified.
3. Follow-Up Sent.
4. Pilot Candidate.
5. Repeat Contact.

Assign one owner.

Set one follow-up deadline.

Define one pilot rule.

After the event, run a 30-minute review:

What did we learn about Vietnam that we did not know before?

## Decision Rule

Do not call an event successful until the follow-up is complete.

The event ends only when every qualified Studio has one of four statuses:

- Pilot discussion;
- Nurture path;
- Not now;
- Referral to a better-fit partner.

This changes behavior.

The Publisher prepares better questions.

The Studio prepares better materials.

The organizer designs better matching.

The ecosystem receives better information.

That is when an event becomes infrastructure.

Without this rule, everyone can leave the room feeling positive while the market forgets what it just learned.

## Infographic Input

Use a 5-stage event funnel:

Attendance -> Qualified Lead -> Follow-Up -> Pilot -> Repeat.

Add success meters:

- Trust Created;
- Signal Captured;
- Opportunities Converted;
- Learning Stored.

Attendance is not the metric.

Conversion is.

Events are not branding if they create a better market memory.

Final operating note:

If I were advising a Publisher entering Vietnam, I would treat every event as a test of local Operating Discipline.

Can the team prepare before the room?

Can it read weak and strong signals during the room?

Can it follow up after the room?

Can it convert the event into better market knowledge?

That is the difference between showing up and building presence.
