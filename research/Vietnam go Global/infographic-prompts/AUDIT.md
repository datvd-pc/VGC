## Visual Standard

Use the Article 18 test image direction as the default:

- Bright warm ivory or light editorial background.
- High-contrast black / charcoal text.
- Bright red title banner.
- Electric cyan dividers and layout frames.
- Neon green stat highlights and score bars.
- Amber accents for warnings, rules, and decision boxes.
- Premium printed gaming magazine feel.
- Retro arcade / 16-bit structure, but not overloaded.
- Fewer icons, more white space, larger text.

Do not default to dark dashboards. Use dark UI only if a future prompt explicitly needs a control-room mood and the text remains highly readable.

## Author And QR Footer Standard

Every infographic prompt must reserve a small footer for attribution and connection:

- Left side footer text: `Author: Dat Dao | linkedin.com/in/datvd90`.
- Bottom-right reserved white square placeholder labeled `QR Code`.
- The QR area must have enough quiet-zone margin for a real QR code to be added later.
- The image generator must not create a fake scannable QR code.
- Footer must stay secondary; it should not compete with the main hook, formula, or decision rule.

Important LinkedIn caveat:

QR is useful for desktop viewers, reshared images, saved images, and screenshots. It is weaker as the only CTA for mobile viewers because they are already viewing the image on the same phone. For that reason, the footer must also include the readable LinkedIn handle / URL as text.

## Information Density Rule

Each infographic should be useful even if the viewer does not read the article.

Minimum:

- 1 sharp headline.
- 1 viewer-benefit subtitle.
- 3-7 core labels.
- 1 decision rule or formula.
- 1 bottom takeaway.
- 1 visual structure that teaches the idea.

Maximum:

- No more than 4 large icon groups.
- No more than 8 small labels unless they are grouped cleanly.
- No dense node maps.
- No tiny explanatory text.
- No decorative clutter that competes with the argument.
