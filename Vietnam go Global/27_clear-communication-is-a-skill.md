# Clear Communication Is A Skill

Communication is not a soft skill.
It is a control skill.

That becomes obvious very quickly when a game team works with a Publisher, a foreign partner, or even another internal role that does not share the same assumptions.

Many mistakes in game production are not caused by low intelligence or weak effort.
They happen because someone thought they had aligned, but they had only spoken.

That is a very different thing.

In practice, clear communication saves time, money, and trust.

The formula is:

> Communication = purpose + questions + assumptions + decisions + written confirmation

Before the meeting, clarity begins with:

- Why the meeting exists;
- What questions need answers;
- What assumptions are already being made;
- What decision is actually needed.

During the meeting, clarity means:

- Repeating back what you heard;
- Naming the owner;
- Naming the deadline;
- Naming the risk;
- Naming what is still uncertain.

After the meeting, clarity means:

- Writing the summary;
- Writing the action items;
- Confirming the owner;
- Confirming the next step;
- Leaving a trail that others can read later.

This matters even more when working with foreign partners. English is not the real issue. The real issue is explicit alignment.

If you do not write it down, different people will remember different things.
If you do not repeat it back, you may be agreeing to something you did not actually mean.

That is why communication should be treated as risk control, not as politeness.

If I were a PM, I would not let a meeting end without written recap.
If I were a junior Developer, I would ask for clearer requirements before starting.
If I were a Founder, I would make sure the team knows how to turn conversations into records.

In the next 2-4 years, the teams that communicate clearly will move faster than the teams that only work hard.

Hard work matters.
But clarity compounds.

For Vietnamese talent, this is one of the fastest ways to look more senior without pretending.

You do not need perfect English to communicate well.
You need clear thinking, written structure, and the habit of confirming meaning.

The common problem when working with Western Partners is not only “I do not understand English.”

The real problem is:

- I understood part of the message;
- I guessed the missing part;
- I did not ask because I was afraid of looking weak;
- I said yes because the call was moving fast;
- I started work based on an assumption;
- The partner expected something else.

This is how many projects lose time.

The fix is not complicated.

Before a meeting, send a short note:

> Purpose: confirm the test scope for Build 0.2.  
> Questions: should onboarding be playable or only mocked?  
> Assumption: we are optimizing for first-session clarity, not content volume.  
> Risk: if we add the meta layer now, the build may slip by 3 days.  
> Decision needed: cut meta layer or move test date.

That one note changes the quality of the meeting.

It tells the Publisher that you are not just waiting for instructions.
It tells your team what to prepare.
It gives everyone a shared starting point.

During the meeting, use simple confirmation phrases:

> Let me repeat to make sure I understand.  
> So the priority is clarity, not feature count. Correct?  
> We can do A by Friday, but B will need two more days. Which one matters more?  
> This is still unclear to me. Can we decide it now or move it to the next checkpoint?

These phrases are not weak.

They are professional.

After the meeting, send the recap:

> Summary: we agreed to keep the build focused on the core loop.  
> Decision: meta layer is postponed.  
> Owner: Dev team owns playable build, PM owns event checklist.  
> Deadline: Friday 5 PM Vietnam time.  
> Risk: onboarding may still need simplification after review.  
> Next step: send build and 5-minute gameplay video.

This is the habit many Vietnamese juniors need to learn early.

It also protects managers.

If there is a later disagreement, the recap becomes the decision trail. People can correct it quickly. Nobody needs to rely on memory.

AI can help, but only if you use it with judgment.

Good AI prompt:

> Turn these rough meeting notes into a short professional recap. Keep decisions, owners, deadlines, open questions, and risks. Do not add anything that was not discussed.

Then check the output yourself.

AI should make your communication clearer, not fake certainty.

The prediction:

As teams become more distributed and AI increases production speed, communication will become a stronger filter. The slow team will not always be the team with weaker Developers. Sometimes it will be the team with unclear decisions.

For Vietnamese talent, this is good news.

Clear communication is learnable.

And in a global market, learnable skills that create trust are extremely valuable.

There is also a career signal here.

People often think seniority comes from speaking less or sounding more certain.
That is not enough.

Real senior communication does three things:

- It makes the decision visible;
- It makes the risk visible;
- It makes the next step visible.

That is why the strongest communicators are often not the loudest ones.

They are the ones who can compress confusion into something the team can act on.

If you are a junior, practice this structure every time:

> Purpose -> Question -> Assumption -> Risk -> Decision

If you are a PM, use it to manage meetings.
If you are a Developer or Designer, use it to ask for clarity.
If you are a Founder, use it to keep the team honest.

That one structure alone will lift the quality of many Vietnamese teams.
