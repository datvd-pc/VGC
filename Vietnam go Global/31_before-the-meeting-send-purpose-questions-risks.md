# Before The Meeting, Send Purpose, Questions, Risks

Good meetings start before the meeting.

That sounds simple.
It is also one of the most useful habits a Vietnamese team can build when working with foreign partners.

Too many meetings begin with no agenda, no clear decision needed, and no visible risk. That is how time gets wasted.

The formula is:

> Meeting prep = purpose + questions + risks + decision needed

Before the meeting, you should know:

- Why the meeting exists;
- What needs to be decided;
- Which questions need answers;
- Which assumptions are already being made;
- What could go wrong if nobody is careful.

Then send that in advance.

This helps the other side prepare properly.
It also forces you to think clearly before you start speaking.

AI can help here in a very practical way.

You can use it to:

- Draft the agenda;
- Turn a rough thought into a clean question list;
- Scan a brief for missing points;
- Rewrite the message so it is short and direct.

That saves time.
More importantly, it reduces ambiguity.

If I were a PM, I would never book an important meeting without a purpose statement.
If I were a Founder, I would ask for the same thing from my team.
If I were a junior, I would learn to write down what I am trying to resolve before I talk.

The next 2-4 years will reward people who can prepare better than others.
It is not glamorous, but it works.

Preparation is respect.
Preparation is also risk control.

This habit is especially important when working with Western Partners.

Many Vietnamese teams enter meetings in listening mode. They wait for the Publisher, Client, or senior person to explain what needs to be done. That can feel respectful, but in a professional cross-border setting it may be read as passivity.

A strong team prepares the room before the room starts.

The pre-meeting note should be short.
It should not be a long document.
It should help people think before they speak.

Use this structure:

> Purpose  
> Current Context  
> Questions  
> Assumptions  
> Risks  
> Decisions Needed

Example:

> Purpose: align on the next playable build for the Hybrid Puzzle prototype.  
> Current Context: core loop is playable, but onboarding is still too long.  
> Questions: should we optimize for first-session clarity or add meta progression now?  
> Assumption: the next test is mainly for D1 and first-session comprehension, not long-term Monetization.  
> Risks: adding meta now may delay the build by 3-4 days and create noise in the test.  
> Decisions Needed: cut meta for this build, or delay test to include it.

This does three things.

First, it shows ownership.

You are not waiting passively. You are helping the meeting become useful.

Second, it reduces misunderstanding.

If the assumption is wrong, the partner can correct it before the team wastes time.

Third, it improves power balance.

Even if you are a smaller Studio, clear preparation makes you look like an Operator, not only a vendor.

AI can help draft this note from rough context.

Prompt:

> Turn these project notes into a pre-meeting brief for a Publisher PM. Keep it short. Include Purpose, Context, Questions, Assumptions, Risks, and Decisions Needed. Use direct professional English.

Then edit it yourself.

Do not send AI output blindly.

The human responsibility is judgment:

- Is the risk real?
- Is the question specific?
- Is the decision needed now?
- Is the tone direct but respectful?
- Is the note short enough to read?

This is the kind of smart work young Vietnamese talent should practice.

It is not glamorous.
It is not a viral skill.
But it makes work smoother, faster, and more trusted.

In a market where many people can build, the person who makes decisions clearer becomes valuable.

One more useful habit:

If the meeting is important, send the note in a format that is easy to answer.

For example:

> Purpose: confirm scope for Friday build.  
> Questions: 1) Is meta in or out? 2) Is tutorial fixed or still in review?  
> Risks: if we add one more feature, the test may lose clarity.  
> Decision needed: cut now or delay 2 days.

Short notes get answers.
Long notes often get ignored.

That is why this skill is partly editorial.

You are not only writing.
You are designing the response you want.

That is a real professional skill.
