# 21 - Deal Structure Decides Learning

## Goal
Create a LinkedIn infographic that makes foreign Publishers and Vietnamese Studio founders understand one sharp idea: the structure of a deal decides what the Studio learns, keeps, and can reuse after the project ends.

## Viewer Benefit
The viewer should immediately get a practical deal-review checklist. A Publisher sees how to design partnerships that create better local operators. A Studio founder sees why a higher revenue share is not enough if the deal leaves no Data, no process, no reusable assets, and no stronger team capability.

## Core Message
A deal is not only a money split. It is a learning architecture.

If the project fails, the Studio should still gain something: better production process, clearer Data literacy, reusable systems, documented postmortems, stronger communication habits, and a more credible next deal.

## Data Points To Include
- 4 value buckets: Cash Value, Asset Value, Learning Value, Relationship Value.
- 6 learning moments: Scope, Prototype, Soft Launch, UA Test, Live Ops, Postmortem.
- 1 key question: “If the project fails, what does the Studio still own?”
- 2022-2026 context: rising CPI and tougher hit discovery make capability transfer more valuable than one-off revenue share.
- 2026-2029 implication: Studios that learn through deals compound faster than Studios that only chase the next project.

## Text On Image
Main title: “Deal Structure Decides Learning”

Subtitle: “Revenue Share Pays Later. Capability Compounds Earlier.”

Central question: “If The Game Fails, What Does The Studio Still Own?”

Four large blocks:
- Cash Value: Milestones, Revenue Share, Recoup Terms.
- Asset Value: Source Code, Tools, Templates, Reusable Pipelines.
- Learning Value: Data Review, UA Feedback, Live Ops Decisions.

Bottom formula:
“Better Deal = Money + Assets + Data + Learning Rights”

Bottom takeaway:
“Money Pays The Month. Learning Compounds The Company.”

Do not make a dark dashboard. Do not use too many tiny icons. Do not create legal-document clutter. Avoid long paragraphs, tiny text, gray-on-gray colors, and dense arrows.

## Quality Check
The image must be readable at phone size. The first-glance hook must be the central question. The viewer should understand in 5 seconds that better deals must transfer capability, not only money.
