# If I Were A Vietnamese Founder With Cashflow But No Hit

Cashflow is useful only if it buys capability.

That is the honest answer for many Vietnamese Founders.

If your Studio has revenue but no hit, you are not in a failure state. You are in a choice state.

The choice is:

- Keep everything comfortable;
- Or use the Cashflow to buy a better future.

The second path is harder.
The first path is easier.

That is why many Founders stop too early.

They have enough money to live.
They have enough reputation to breathe.
They have enough work to stay busy.
And then they lose urgency.

That is not irrational.
It is human.

But if you want to keep growing, the right use of Cashflow is not comfort. It is capability purchase.

What should you buy?

- Better analytics;
- One strong PM or producer;
- Economy expertise;
- Live Ops support;
- Legal clarity;
- A better review system;
- More time to test.

The formula is:

> Cashflow -> capability purchase -> better odds

If I were a Founder, I would ask:

- What is this month’s Cashflow buying?
- Is it comfort or leverage?
- Is it keeping me alive or making me stronger?

If I were a Publisher, I would want to see whether the Founder uses Cashflow to build or just to relax.

In the next 2-4 years, teams that use stable Cashflow to improve their Operating System will outlast teams that only use Cashflow to stay comfortable.

Cashflow is not the destination.
It is the tool.

The hardest part is psychological, not strategic.

Cashflow can make a Founder comfortable. In Vietnam, a modestly successful game business can create income that is already far above the local average. That changes motivation. A Founder may not feel the same pressure to chase a global hit if the current business already supports a good life, pays the team, and avoids major debt.

This is not laziness.

It is incentive design.

If the upside of going global is uncertain, but the pain is immediate, many people will choose the path that protects what they already have.

That is rational.

But the Founder should name it honestly.

If you want a lifestyle Cashflow company, build it well. Protect margin. Keep scope simple. Avoid unnecessary complexity. Build a stable hiring model. Do not pretend you are trying to become the next global Publisher.

If you want to become an operating Studio, accept that the company must change.

You will need better reporting.
You will need stronger middle management.
You will need Data discipline.
You will need sharper product reviews.
You will need clearer deal negotiation.
You will need to let some talented people challenge your instinct.
You will need to survive projects that are strategically useful but financially painful in the short term.

That is a different game.

The danger for many Studios is staying in the middle.

They want global recognition, but manage like a small workshop.
They want better deals, but do not build negotiation leverage.
They want Publisher respect, but do not prepare Data.
They want talent loyalty, but do not show a learning path.
They want young people to be ambitious, but give them only production tasks.

This middle zone is where energy leaks.

If I were the Founder, I would create a simple internal scorecard:

- Cash runway;
- Monthly burn;
- Revenue concentration;
- Prototype output;
- Hit-rate by test type;
- D1, D7, playtime, ARPDAU benchmarks where available;
- Team seniority by function;
- Number of people who can make decisions without me;
- Number of reusable systems;
- Number of Publisher relationships that trust me without a middleman.

This scorecard would tell me what kind of company I actually have.

Then I would choose one of three 12-month paths.

Path one: **Defend Cashflow**.
This means focus on low-risk projects, polish, reskin, operational efficiency, and team stability. The goal is not glory. The goal is durability.

Path two: **Buy Learning**.
This means using Cashflow to fund one capability upgrade: Data, Game Design, AI pipeline, Producer system, or Publisher relationship. The goal is to become better, not immediately bigger.

Path three: **Build For Global Operation**.
This means accepting more risk, hiring stronger leaders, building Data and Live Ops capability, and negotiating deals that transfer learning.

The wrong move is to choose path three in public and path one in daily decisions.

Teams can feel that inconsistency.
Publishers can feel it too.

In the next 2-4 years, I think many Vietnamese Studios will not fail because they cannot build games. They will struggle because they do not choose the right operating identity.

The best Founders will be clear:

“We are a Cashflow Studio.”
“We are a prototype partner.”
“We are building toward global operation.”

Each answer can work.

But each answer requires a different company.

If I were that Founder, I would turn the choice into a written policy:

> For the next 12 months, we are choosing one operating identity.

Then I would make the team feel that choice in real life.

If we are a Cashflow Studio, we optimize for margin, repeatability, and team stability.
If we are a prototype partner, we optimize for speed, learning, and clean test design.
If we are building toward global operation, we optimize for Data, communication, legal clarity, and leadership depth.

The worst state is pretending to be all three at once.

That usually creates mediocre execution in every direction.

The real value of Cashflow is not comfort.
It is optionality.

Use it to buy a better version of the company, not only a safer version of the same company.

That is the cleanest summary:

Cashflow should buy options, not excuses.
