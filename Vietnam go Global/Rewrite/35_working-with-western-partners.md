# Working With Western Partners

Working with Western Partners is not mostly about English. It is about explicit alignment.

> Western working = explicit prep + explicit confirm + explicit trail

The most common misunderstanding is agreement. In many Vietnamese teams, "yes" means "I heard you" or "I respect the direction." In Western business settings, "yes" often means "I agree, I understand, I can deliver, and I accept responsibility." Those are not the same.

Separate the meanings:

- "I understand."
- "I agree."
- "I can deliver."
- "I need to check."
- "I see a risk."
- "I disagree because..."

Before every important decision, ask: "Are we confirming direction, or are we confirming commitment?"

Use "confirm / clarify / propose" instead of only "yes / no":

- Confirm: "I understand the scope."
- Clarify: "I want to check the deadline."
- Propose: "I suggest we cut this part for now."

Respectful pushback means making tradeoffs visible: "We can do this, but it will affect the test date." "If the priority is speed, I recommend cutting this part." "I may be missing context, but here is my concern."

For Western Partners: do not assume silence means alignment, politeness means confidence, or fast execution means deep understanding. Ask the Vietnamese team to write back what they understood, what they will do, what risk they see, and what they need confirmed.

> The best partnership is not where both sides talk more. It is where both sides misunderstand less.
