# The Yes Problem

In Vietnam, "yes" does not always mean alignment. This is one of the most expensive misunderstandings in cross-border game work.

"Yes" can mean: I heard you, I roughly agree, I want to avoid conflict, or I will do it exactly as you asked. Those are not the same thing.

> Alignment = repeat-back + written decision + risk escalation

Before the meeting: send purpose, questions, risks, and decisions needed. During the meeting: repeat back decisions, confirm owner, confirm deadline, name uncertainty. After the meeting: write the summary, confirm next steps, confirm what is still open.

The simplest habit that solves most of this: after every important conversation, ask the team to write three lines:

1. What do we think we agreed on?
2. What are we still unsure about?
3. What will we do next?

This uncovers hidden misalignment fast. It also works well with AI: turn messy meeting notes into a short recap, then ask the team to validate it.

> Do you mean yes to the direction, yes to the scope, or yes to the deadline?

That question makes the response concrete. Written confirmation fixes much of the gap between intention and execution. It tells the other side you care about accuracy, not just harmony.
