# If I Were Interviewing A Vietnamese PM

A PM is not just someone who keeps tasks moving. In games, a PM must keep the outcome moving.

> Good PM = ownership + clarity + escalation + outcome thinking

I would not begin with "Do you know Jira?" I would begin with: Tell me about the last project that failed. What did you do when the team started drifting? How did you escalate risk? What did you do before the deadline slipped? What did you learn that you can reuse?

A good PM makes ambiguity smaller, tells the truth early, keeps the team aligned, knows what to push and what to stop, and understands the business consequence of delay.

The PM must understand power. In a game team, the Publisher wants speed and Portfolio fit, the Founder wants Cashflow and reputation, the Developer wants stable scope, the Designer wants room to test, the Artist wants clear direction. If the PM only tries to please everyone, the project becomes unclear.

Written communication is a management tool. Before a meeting: purpose, questions, decisions needed, risks. After a meeting: summary, decisions, open questions, owners, deadlines, changed scope, next checkpoint.

> Show me the recap. Show me the risk log. Show me how you corrected the misunderstanding.

That tells me more than a confident introduction. A CV can exaggerate. Documents are harder to fake.

Behavioral questions: "Tell me about a time you had to send a difficult update in writing" reveals how they think about truth and write under pressure. "What is the worst misunderstanding you have had with a Publisher?" reveals whether they can learn from cross-border friction.

In 2026-2029, as AI increases production speed, the bottleneck moves toward clarity, judgment, and coordination. The PM who connects Business, Product, Data, People, and Execution becomes a leverage point.
