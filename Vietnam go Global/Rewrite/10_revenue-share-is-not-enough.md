# Revenue Share Is Not Enough

Revenue Share sounds like success. For a Vietnamese Studio, it can be. But it is not enough.

A Studio can make real money from a hit and still end up with very little long-term power. That happens when the Studio gets paid for success but does not keep enough of the learning, the rights, or the Operating Control.

A hit creates cash. It does not automatically create a company that can repeat the result.

In Vietnam, a hit can already change a Founder's life. Some teams will cash out. Some will become lifestyle Studios. Some will chase the next hit. Very few will reinvest into Operating Maturity.

> Revenue Share rewards success. It does not automatically build maturity.

For Publishers, revenue alone does not tell you whether the Studio will learn from Data, keep improving systems, retain key staff, build Live Ops muscle, or reuse learning on the next title.

The real questions beyond payout: Who owns the IP? Who owns the source? Who owns the dashboards and post-launch learning? Who decides scale or kill? What rights does the Studio keep? What can be reused?

> A hit reveals product-market fit. It does not reveal company-market fit.

If you only share Revenue Share, you are sharing one outcome. If you share Data, you share understanding. If you share source access, you share future reuse. If you share postmortems, you share learning. If you share IP, you share long-term value.

A good Revenue Share deal should help both sides become better at the next deal. If it does not, it is only a temporary financial arrangement.

> Does this deal make the next project easier to build, easier to understand, or easier to scale? If no, the deal is probably too thin.
