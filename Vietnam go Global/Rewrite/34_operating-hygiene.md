# Operating Hygiene

A strong team is not the team that does the most. It is the team that remembers the best.

> Operating hygiene = memory + cadence + accountability

The minimum set: Notes, Decision log, Risk log, Postmortem, Handoff, Weekly review. Each item has a purpose - notes keep the trace, decision logs explain why choices were made, risk logs show uncertainty, postmortems stop repeat mistakes, handoffs prevent work from falling through cracks, weekly reviews keep the team learning.

In many Vietnamese Studios, the informal style works internally but breaks when working with a foreign Publisher who cannot see the room or read local context.

> Important information should not live only in someone's head.

Minimum system for a young Studio:

**Daily** - update blockers, flag scope changes, record major decisions.
**Weekly** - review milestone, review risks, review Data or playtest findings, confirm next decision.
**After every build** - record what changed, what broke, what was learned, what should be reused.

Each item needs one owner. "Everyone" often means "no one." When the team gets busy, hygiene is the first thing to vanish - exactly when it matters most.

AI can reduce the cost: clean meeting notes, format risk logs, compare scope with original brief, generate QA checklists, summarize weekly progress, prepare postmortem structure. But someone still needs to check whether the note is true, decide what matters, and follow up when risk appears.

> Can we still explain what happened last week without asking three people separately?

If the answer is no, the system is too weak.

In the next 2-4 years, Operating Hygiene will become a bigger advantage because faster building also creates faster confusion if the Operating System is weak.
