# PM / Founder With Runway Short

When runway is short, bad priority costs more than bad effort.

> Short runway = smaller bets + faster learning + earlier kills

The hardest part is not deciding what to build. It is deciding what not to build.

Use a 90-day survival map:

**Step 1 - Calculate the truth:** current cash, monthly burn, committed revenue, likely revenue, unpaid invoices, minimum team cost, realistic runway.

**Step 2 - Classify projects:** pays now, may pay soon, teaches something important, builds leverage, only consumes attention.

**Step 3 - Choose operating mode:**
- **Survival mode** - protect cash, cut scope.
- **Learning mode** - spend carefully to buy capability.
- **Growth mode** - take bigger bets only when the base is stable.

Do not confuse them. The danger is panic - chasing too many options at once (one service project, one Publisher pitch, one prototype, one reskin, one original IP). That looks active but destroys focus.

Make tradeoffs visible: If we accept this service job, what project slows down? If we keep this prototype, what salary risk increases? If we hire this person, what capability do they unlock? If we chase this Publisher, what proof do we have?

> The goal is not only to survive the next 90 days. The goal is to survive in a way that improves the next 180 days.

In Vietnam, lean costs can hide weak strategy. A team can stay alive for a long time without becoming stronger. Under short runway, clarity is worth more than motion. If a project does not help survival or learning, it is probably too costly.
