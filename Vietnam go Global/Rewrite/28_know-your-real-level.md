# Know Your Real Level

The cost of overestimating yourself is not pride. It is wasted time, failed delivery, and broken trust.

> Real level = output + reliability + learning speed + ownership

Real level is not a feeling. It is a pattern. Ask: Can I deliver output? Can I do it repeatedly? Can I handle pressure? Can I fix it when it fails?

Experience asks: "What have I been near?" Ownership asks: "What did I actually decide, deliver, fix, and improve?" Those are different.

Self-check by role:

- **Developer:** Can I estimate accurately? Explain tradeoffs to non-technical people? Build reusable systems? Prevent bugs through structure?
- **Designer:** Can I connect mechanic to player behavior? Define what the prototype is testing? Cut features without losing the core? Read Retention and session behavior?
- **PM:** Can I make ambiguity smaller? Identify risk before the deadline? Push back without damaging trust? Turn meetings into decisions?
- **Founder:** Can the company operate when I am not in every decision? Do we have Data discipline, legal clarity, and cash runway awareness?

A powerful test: what would still work if you were absent? If the answer is "not much," your level is still lower than you may feel.

Confidence is easy. Accuracy is leverage. The best people know exactly where they are strong, where they are weak, and what they are doing about it.
