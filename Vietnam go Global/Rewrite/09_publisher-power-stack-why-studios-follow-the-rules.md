# Publisher Power Stack: Why Studios Follow The Rules

Studio talent is real. Publisher power is still bigger. That is a structural statement, not a moral one.

A strong Publisher usually controls five layers that matter in Puzzle and Hybrid Puzzle:

1. **Capital** - pays for UA, milestone support, burn rate, and soft-launch risk.
2. **Big Data** - better read on creative, cohort quality, and game performance across many titles.
3. **Legal leverage** - decides who owns IP, source code, test Data, exclusivity, sequel rights, and publishing rights.
4. **Distribution** - decides who can actually scale the game.
5. **Operating System** - decides whether the Publisher can make the game better after launch.

> Publisher power = capital + Big Data + legal + distribution + Operating System

A Revenue Share deal can feel fair on paper and still leave the Studio with very little power in practice. If the Publisher controls Data, rights, and scale decisions, the Studio may win cash but lose learning.

The important questions are not only about Revenue Share, advance, or UA budget. You also need to ask: Who owns the Data? Who owns the source? Who owns the creative learning? Who decides scale or kill? What learning does the Studio retain?

This is the difference between selling output and selling capability. If a Studio is only selling output, the Publisher can replace it. If a Studio is selling capability, the Publisher has a reason to stay.

The best Studios should not just negotiate revenue. They should negotiate learning rights: access to dashboards, postmortem rights, reusable insights, sequel rights, and clarity on IP ownership.

> The same contract can either build a partner or keep a supplier.

In the next 2-4 years, AI will make the supply of prototypes larger, giving Publishers more choice. The Studio that keeps learning will have a real advantage.

A Studio can earn enough to feel strong and still have little real leverage. The healthy question is: what does the Studio keep after launch?
