# After The Meeting, Send Summary, Owners, Next Steps

If there is no summary, the meeting may as well not have happened.

> Meeting output = summary + owner + deadline + risk + next step

Template:

> Summary: what was decided.
> Decisions: what changed.
> Owners: who owns each item.
> Deadlines: when it is due.
> Open Questions: what is still uncertain.
> Risks: what could go wrong.
> Next Step: what happens next.

"Please correct me if I missed anything" gives the other side an easy way to fix misunderstanding without creating conflict.

Without written recap, people remember different things. A Vietnamese teammate may avoid direct disagreement in the call. A Western Partner may assume silence means agreement. A Developer may remember only the technical constraint. A Designer may remember only the feature direction.

Bad recap: "We discussed the build. Team will update soon."

Better recap: "Build 0.3 will focus on core loop readability. Meta progression is postponed until after the next playtest. Dev team owns playable build, Designer owns onboarding revision, PM owns event checklist. Deadline: Friday 5 PM Vietnam time. Risk: current tutorial may still be too long."

AI can create the first draft from rough notes. Then check: did AI invent anything? Are owners named clearly? Are deadlines specific? Are risks visible? Can a person who missed the meeting understand what happened?

The recap is not clerical work. It is operating leverage. In 2026-2029, as work moves faster and teams handle more tools, Data, versions, and decisions, the bottleneck will be keeping everyone aligned while making things.
