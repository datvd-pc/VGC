# Deal Structure Decides Learning

Most people think a good deal is about revenue split. For Vietnamese Studios, the deeper question is what the deal teaches the team after launch.

A Studio can earn money from a strong Rev-Share deal and still come out weaker if it loses access to Data, source, post-launch learning, or the right to reuse what it learned.

> Deal = money + learning + control + exit rules

There are four things a Studio can own after a project:

1. **Cash Value** - production fee, minimum guarantee, Revenue Share.
2. **Asset Value** - code, tools, art pipeline, templates, IP rights.
3. **Learning Value** - Data access, test results, postmortem, creative performance.
4. **Relationship Value** - trust, next-project option, better negotiation position.

Weak teams only see Cash Value. Better teams see Cash and Asset Value. Strong Operators negotiate for all four.

A practical learning loop: shared hypothesis and kill criteria before production, milestone review during, agreed event map before test, Data review and written postmortem after test, clear reason for scale/iterate/kill after decision, and reusable lessons after project.

> A high Revenue Share on a blind deal can still be a bad deal.

Money pays the month. Learning compounds the company.

If this project fails, what exactly will the Studio still own? If the answer is nothing useful, the deal is too thin.
