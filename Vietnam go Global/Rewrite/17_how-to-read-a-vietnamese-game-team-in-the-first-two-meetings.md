# How To Read A Vietnamese Game Team In The First Two Meetings

The first two meetings tell you more than most people think. They will not tell you everything, but they are enough to read whether a team is selling output, speed, or operating capability.

Key questions that reveal how a team thinks: What Cashflow path are you on? What was the last failed project and what did you learn? What does the team keep after each prototype? Who owns Data, decision, and follow-up? What happens when a project looks weak? How do you decide kill, iterate, or scale?

> First two meetings = Cashflow path + learning path + control path

If the team only talks about what they built, you are reading output. If they talk about what they learned, you are reading maturity. If they talk about how they operate when things get messy, you are reading potential.

Good signs: direct answers, can name what they do not know, can explain failures, can discuss process without defensiveness, can say no when necessary.

Risky signs: polished but vague answers, no clear owner, cannot explain failures, cannot separate wish from evidence, says yes to everything too quickly.

Leave the first meeting with three notes: how they make money today, how they learn from failure, and how much control they are ready to share.

Watch for three small signals: how the team handles uncertainty (asks clear questions or guesses quietly), how they handle correction (defensive or adaptive), and how they handle ownership (who makes the call).

The first two meetings are not about charm. They are about risk reading. Readable teams are easier to trust, easier to steer, and easier to work with over time.
