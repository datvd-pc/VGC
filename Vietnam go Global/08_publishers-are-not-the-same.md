# Publishers Are Not The Same

Many Studios pitch every Publisher as if they were the same buyer. They are not.

That mistake is expensive.

Voodoo, SayGames, Supersonic, Rollic, Homa, TapNation, CrazyLabs, and Lion/AppLovin do not play the same game. They all sit inside mobile publishing, but their strategy, risk model, and selection logic are different.

That means a Vietnamese Studio should not present the same way to everyone.

Here is the simplest way to see the difference:

- Voodoo leans toward industrialized filtering and high-volume prototype testing.
- SayGames leans toward fewer bets and deeper partnerships.
- Supersonic leans toward transparency, Data, and Unity-integrated publishing.
- Rollic leans toward hybrid puzzle craftsmanship and deeper long-term titles.
- Homa leans toward tool-assisted hybridization and portfolio expansion.
- TapNation leans toward broad Studio networks and infrastructure-heavy scaling.
- CrazyLabs leans toward broad funnel Monetization and the long tail.
- Lion/AppLovin leans toward platform-backed publishing with a strong growth stack.

These are not minor differences. They change what each Publisher needs from you.

For example:

If you pitch Voodoo, they may care about speed, creative volume, and whether your prototype can survive filtering.
If you pitch SayGames, they may care more about whether your team can build with them over time.
If you pitch Supersonic, they may care about KPI clarity and whether your team can actually use transparent Data.
If you pitch Rollic, they may care about craftsmanship in level design, pacing, and Retention.

The formula is not:

> Good game + good Publisher = success

The real formula is:

> Game signal + team maturity + Publisher bottleneck = fit

That is a very different conversation.

In practice, foreign Publishers are not only buying prototypes. They are buying optionality. They want to know:

- Can this team produce enough signal?
- Can this team learn fast enough?
- Can this team hold quality after soft launch?
- Can this team become a repeatable partner?

The wrong fit wastes time on both sides.
The right fit can turn a small team into a reliable operating partner.

That is why a Vietnamese Studio should never ask only, “Which Publisher is biggest?” Better questions are:

- Which Publisher’s bottleneck matches our strength?
- Which Publisher needs our kind of output?
- Which Publisher can actually help us grow?
- Which Publisher will keep the learning with us?

This is also where many Studios lose leverage. They pitch one way to everyone. But the market is segmented. The partner strategy should be segmented too.

If I were a Founder, I would prepare different stories for different Publisher archetypes:

- Volume filtering;
- Deep partnership;
- Platform-backed publishing;
- Craftsmanship-led hybrid;
- Long-tail Monetization.

If I were a Publisher, I would want the Studio to understand my model before asking for capital.

In the next 2-4 years, Publisher differentiation will matter even more. AI will make raw production easier. That means the real value will move toward the Publisher’s operating model, not just the fact that they can write checks.

The better your fit, the better your deal.

If you are talking to every Publisher the same way, you are probably leaving money and trust on the table.

There is a second practical layer here for Vietnamese teams.

Different Publishers also imply different kinds of internal work.

If a Publisher is volume-driven, your team should be ready to:

- Test quickly;
- Absorb feedback fast;
- Produce many variants;
- Accept kill decisions early.

If a Publisher is partnership-driven, your team should be ready to:

- Communicate clearly;
- Share learning honestly;
- Keep written records;
- Think beyond one prototype.

If a Publisher is platform-backed, your team should be ready to:

- Understand the platform stack;
- Use the tools they provide;
- Read the Data they surface;
- Work inside a more formal operating frame.

If a Publisher is focused on hybrid craftsmanship, your team should be ready to:

- Think harder about level design;
- Understand trust curves;
- Keep player memory in mind;
- Avoid shallow mechanic copying.

That is why one deck is not enough.

Your pitch should adapt to the buyer:

- A fast-filter deck;
- A learning-partner deck;
- A platform-deck;
- A hybrid-depth deck;
- A long-tail opportunity deck.

This is especially important for Vietnamese Studios that want to go global. You do not need to become everything for everyone.
You do need to know which type of partner can help your current version of the team.

If you pitch the wrong thing, you may still get a meeting.
But you will waste the relationship.

If you pitch the right thing, the Publisher immediately sees where you fit.
That is much better.

The Publisher map is part of your strategy.
Not an afterthought.

One reason this matters is that each Publisher type changes the kind of company you are building.

If a Studio works mainly with fast test Publishers, it will learn speed, creative discipline, and short feedback loops.

If a Studio works with deeper hybrid partners, it will learn content systems, level quality, and Retention thinking.

If a Studio works with portfolio-driven Publishers, it will learn how to manage probability, not just passion.

If a Studio works with IP-minded or strategic partners, it will learn rights, process, and long-term value.

These are different learning paths.

So the Studio should not ask only, “Who will fund us?”

It should ask:

> What kind of company will this relationship help us become?

That question is much sharper.

It also protects against bad positioning.

For example, a team with weak Data habits should not sell itself as a mature operating partner.
A team with strong production but weak Monetization should not pretend to own scaling.
A team with strong design instincts but limited portfolio evidence should not pitch like a proven global operator.

That does not mean hiding your ambition.

It means matching the pitch to the evidence.

The market respects a team that knows exactly what it can and cannot do.

It does not respect vague confidence.

If I were a Vietnamese Founder, I would keep a simple table for every Publisher:

- What they optimize for;
- What they punish;
- What they reward;
- What they expect after the first call;
- What kind of proof makes them move;
- What kind of team they like to back.

That table helps you stop wasting meetings.

It also helps you decide which Publisher should be your first contact for a new idea.

In a crowded market, the wrong Publisher relationship can consume months of time.
The right one can compress learning dramatically.

That is why Publishers are not the same.

If you want a short decision rule, use this:

- Fast filter: great for speed and volume.
- Hybrid depth: great for retention and craft.
- Platform-backed: great for operating structure.
- IP strategy: great for long-term value.
- Long-tail monetization: great for breadth and portfolio play.

The question is not which one is best.
The question is which one matches your current team.
