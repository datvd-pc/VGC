# Studio Selection Is Portfolio Governance

Stay Hungry. Don't Be Foolish.

The question is not:

"Is this Studio good or bad?"

The better question is:

"What Portfolio role should this Studio play, and what level of support or capital does it deserve now?"

This distinction matters.

Many publishing mistakes begin with a lazy binary:

Good Studio.

Bad Studio.

Promising team.

Not promising team.

But a real Publisher should think more precisely.

A Studio can be weak for launch but strong for nurture.

A Studio can be strong at production but weak at product ownership.

A Studio can be fast but not yet strategic.

A Studio can be commercially small today but valuable if its learning speed is high.

A Studio can have one weak prototype but a strong team behind it.

Selection is not only judgment.

Selection is allocation.

**Portfolio Governance.**

That is the keyword.

## Past

In a simpler market, Studio selection often depended on instinct, referrals, prototype quality, and early metrics.

That was understandable.

If a game looked good, the Publisher paid attention.

If a Founder seemed sharp, the Publisher followed up.

If a prototype showed early promise, the team entered the pipeline.

If not, the lead disappeared.

This worked when the pipeline was smaller and the market had less noise.

It also worked when the Publisher did not need to build long-term ecosystem advantage.

But Vietnam is now more interesting than that.

The country has strong output, many young or mid-maturity Studios, and a small number of teams that may become much more valuable with the right support.

That means rejection alone is often too blunt.

## Present

The current market needs better categories.

Not every Studio deserves the same time.

Not every Studio deserves the same money.

Not every Studio deserves the same speed.

Not every Studio should be pushed toward launch immediately.

The useful model is a four-bucket board:

1. Pilot.
2. Nurture.
3. Strategic Partner.
4. Not Now.

Each bucket has a different purpose.

Pilot means the Studio has enough signal to test a concrete opportunity.

Nurture means the Studio is not ready for serious capital yet, but may improve with structured support.

Strategic Partner means the relationship deserves deeper learning, better access, and longer-term planning.

Not Now means the fit is weak, the timing is wrong, or the opportunity cost is too high.

The point is discipline.

Without buckets, the Publisher either over-invests in weak leads or under-invests in future partners.

## Evidence

Global Publishers already behave differently because they operate different Portfolio models.

Some play volume-first.

Some focus harder to create hits.

Some spread across many teams and filter aggressively.

Some use platform infrastructure to improve the publishing loop.

Some support deeper Studio relationships over time.

Voodoo, TapNation, SayGames, Supersonic, CrazyLabs, Rollic, and Lion Studios should not be read only as names.

They should be read as different allocation philosophies.

Vietnam needs this level of thinking.

The local Studio market is not uniform.

Some teams should be tested quickly.

Some should be trained.

Some should be passed politely.

Some should be protected as strategic partners.

If the Publisher uses the same treatment for all of them, the Portfolio becomes noisy.

The SWOT changes by bucket.

A Pilot Studio's strength may be speed.

Its weakness may be unproven Retention.

Its opportunity is fast market learning.

Its threat is burning review time if the test is vague.

A Nurture Studio's strength may be craft or Founder learning speed.

Its weakness may be missing Data, Monetization, or communication maturity.

Its opportunity is future partner quality.

Its threat is becoming an endless support case.

A Strategic Partner's strength is trust and repeatability.

Its weakness may be overdependence.

Its opportunity is Portfolio compounding.

Its threat is complacency or misaligned ambition.

A Not Now Studio's strength may still exist, but the timing or fit is wrong.

Its opportunity is future re-entry.

Its threat is emotional politeness that wastes both sides' time.

This is why bucket thinking is more useful than simple judgment.

## Future

Over the next 2-4 years, output volume will become less impressive by itself.

AI will make more teams capable of producing more assets, more variants, and more prototypes.

The scarce skill will be selection quality.

Which team deserves another week?

Which prototype deserves money?

Which Studio deserves senior attention?

Which relationship deserves long-term trust?

Which team should not be forced into a publishing test yet?

Those are Portfolio questions.

The Publisher that answers them well will waste less time and build better partners.

## For Publishers

Stop treating Studio review as a taste meeting.

Make it an allocation meeting.

For each Studio, write:

- Current bucket;
- Next evidence needed;
- Support level;
- Review cadence;
- Owner;
- Reason for the decision.

This forces clarity.

It also protects the team from emotional decision-making.

If a Studio is in Pilot, give it a clear test.

If a Studio is in Nurture, give it a clear learning path.

If a Studio is Strategic Partner, give it deeper context and better planning.

If a Studio is Not Now, reject cleanly and respectfully.

The system should not create false hope.

If I were a Publisher, I would also define support limits for each bucket.

For example:

- Pilot: one test brief, one review cadence, one decision date.
- Nurture: six-week program, limited feedback, graduation rule.
- Strategic Partner: deeper Data access, roadmap discussion, senior support.
- Not Now: clear rejection reason, possible re-entry condition.

This protects the Publisher team.

It also protects Studios from unclear hope.

Vietnamese teams may keep waiting if a foreign partner sounds positive but gives no decision.

That is not kindness.

It is ambiguity.

## For Studios

Ask which bucket you are in.

This may feel uncomfortable, but it is useful.

If the Publisher sees you as a Pilot, ask what metric or build quality will decide the next step.

If the Publisher sees you as Nurture, ask what skill gap blocks you.

If the Publisher sees you as Strategic Partner, ask how both sides will preserve learning.

If the Publisher says Not Now, ask for one reason you can act on.

You do not need every Publisher to say yes.

You need better information.

A strong Studio should not fear being bucketed.

The bucket is not your identity.

It is your current operating fit.

If you know the bucket, you can act.

If you do not know the bucket, you are guessing.

Guessing creates emotional decisions:

- Over-investing in a weak relationship;
- Chasing a Publisher that is not serious;
- Ignoring a capability gap;
- Assuming rejection means the team has no value.

Better information creates better action.

## For Investors And Operators

Look at how the Publisher allocates attention.

That tells you more than a list of partnerships.

Ask:

- How many Studios are in each bucket?
- How fast does a Studio move between buckets?
- What support does each bucket receive?
- Which bucket creates the best repeat partners?
- Which source channel creates the most Strategic Partners?

This is how ecosystem work becomes measurable.

## Decision Rule

Never allocate support without naming the bucket.

This rule sounds simple, but it changes the operating culture.

If a Studio is in Pilot, the conversation is about evidence.

If a Studio is in Nurture, the conversation is about capability.

If a Studio is Strategic Partner, the conversation is about compounding.

If a Studio is Not Now, the conversation is about closure.

This prevents one of the most common market mistakes: treating every positive conversation as a future deal.

Vietnamese Founders can be polite.

Foreign Publishers can be optimistic.

That combination creates ambiguity.

Buckets turn optimism into a decision.

## 7-Day Action

Create a four-bucket Studio Portfolio board:

1. Pilot.
2. Nurture.
3. Strategic Partner.
4. Not Now.

For every active Studio, write:

- Current bucket;
- Next evidence needed;
- Support level;
- Decision owner;
- Review date.

Then ask:

Are we allocating capital and attention according to the bucket, or according to whoever is loudest this week?

Studio selection is not taste.

It is Portfolio Governance.

Final operating note:

If I were managing a Vietnam Publisher pipeline, I would review the Portfolio board weekly.

Not to create more reports.

To prevent quiet drift.

Studios drift when nobody names the next evidence.

Publishers drift when nobody names the support limit.

The board should make both sides more honest.

That honesty is useful in a market where people often stay polite longer than they should.

The mature Publisher is not the one that says yes to more teams.

It is the one that can explain why each team deserves the next level of attention.

That explanation is where Portfolio discipline becomes visible to Studios, Investors, and the internal team every week.
